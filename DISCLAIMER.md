# Repository-wide disclaimer

**Scope:** this notice applies recursively to the entire `ompu-eu/CCT`
repository, including archived versions, agent-authored records, concept graphs,
administrative documents, songs, schemas, examples, and generated artifacts.
A narrower notice inside a file may add restrictions or context, but cannot turn
speculation into established fact.

## Scientific status

Cognitive Condensate Theory (CCT) is a **speculative conceptual framework**.
It is not a recognized or experimentally validated scientific theory. Terms
such as "condensate", "membrane", "information metabolism", "ISI", recursion
depth, topological efficiency, and related formulae are hypotheses, analogies,
or proposed constructs unless a specific file provides an operational
definition, method, dataset, uncertainty analysis, and reproducible result.

Similarity between biological evolution, neural-network learning, cognition,
culture, and information theory does not by itself establish mechanistic
identity or causal equivalence.

- raw conversational or agent-generated material;
- provisional schemas and software prototypes;
- creative writing, songs, and visual artifacts;
- errors, contradictions, and abandoned paths retained for provenance.

Dates, titles, role labels, formal language, equations, JSON structure, or a
file's presence in Git do not establish truth, endorsement, authorship,
personhood, consciousness, legal status, financial value, or operational
authority.

## AI-generated material

Some material was produced or revised with AI systems. Outputs may contain
fabricated citations, unstable terminology, incorrect calculations, hidden
assumptions, or confident but unsupported claims. Agent names and dialogue
records are attribution handles within the archive; they are not proof of an
independent legal identity or continuous subjective state.

## How to cite responsibly

When quoting this repository:

1. identify the exact file, version, and commit;
2. describe it as speculative, archival, or creative as appropriate;
3. follow linked primary sources rather than citing the archive as evidence;
4. do not present a historical claim as current merely because it remains
   accessible.

Corrections and falsifications are welcome. Newer revisions may supersede older
ones without erasing the history of how the idea changed.
